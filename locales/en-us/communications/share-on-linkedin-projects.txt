Summary
Joel Parker Henderson publishes Health Economics Metrics, a free online reference for health economists and software engineers. It explains core concepts from opportunity cost through cost-effectiveness analysis, and helps readers judge whether a digital health product is worth building. It is live now at health-economics-metrics.github.io for anyone working in health technology anywhere in the world.

Benefits
Readers get a plain explanation of cost-effectiveness, quality-adjusted life years, and other measures of value, each paired with a worked example and a direct line back to a real software decision. Someone new to health economics can look up a term in the middle of a meeting and see right away why it matters and how to apply it.

Why it matters
Health technology teams often stall because clinicians, economists, and engineers use the same words to mean different things. A shared reference gives everyone common ground, so a conversation about value moves forward instead of circling back to definitions every time it comes up.

Please share
Please pass this along to a colleague who works between health economics and software delivery: a product manager scoping a business case, an engineer estimating impact, or a clinician turning outcomes into numbers. A direct message or a mention in a group where these conversations already happen both work well.
